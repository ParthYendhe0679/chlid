Regulation 10: Registration and document upload within 30 days
A prospective adoptive parent registers on CARINGS and states their preferences: the child's age group, gender and states. The parent must upload the required documents within 30 days of registration. If the documents are not uploaded within 30 days, the registration lapses and the parent must register again.

Regulation 10(8): Home Study Report within 60 days
A social worker from the Specialised Adoption Agency (SAA) or the District Child Protection Unit (DCPU) visits the parent's home and writes the Home Study Report in the official format (Schedule VII). The home study must be completed within 60 days of document submission. The Home Study Report remains valid for 3 years. The Home Study Report fee is Rs 6,000, paid to the agency.

Regulation 9(3): Not reserving a child in 3 referrals
A parent can receive up to 3 referrals, with a gap of about 1 month between referrals. If a parent does not reserve a child in any of their 3 referrals, the parent is debarred, which means blocked, for 1 year. If the refusal was caused by a system error or a justified reason, the parent may keep their place in the queue.

Regulation 11(14): Not accepting a child after reserving
If a parent reserves a child but later does not accept the child, or is found unsuitable, the parent is moved to the bottom of the waiting list. This is a different penalty from the 1-year debarment for not reserving in 3 referrals.

Regulation 37: Child reports and medical certification
The Specialised Adoption Agency uploads the Child Study Report (CSR) and the Medical Examination Report (MER) within 10 days of the child being declared legally free for adoption. The Chief Medical Officer certifies the child as "normal" or "special needs" within 15 days.

Fact 3.3 Referral and the 48-hour reservation
The system offers the parent a child's profile and reports. This is called a referral. The parent must decide whether to reserve the referred child within 48 hours. In February 2025 the Kerala High Court ruled that the 48-hour period should start only after the child's reports are available.

Fact 3.3 Matching, foster care and the adoption order
After reservation, the parent meets the child and the agency's Adoption Committee approves the match within 30 days. The child goes to live with the parents in pre-adoption foster care within 10 days of matching. The agency files the adoption application with the District Magistrate within 10 days of matching. The District Magistrate decides the application within 2 months (60 days) of filing. A certified copy of the order is given to the parents within 10 days, and the child's new birth certificate is issued within 5 days of the order. Since 2022, the District Magistrate issues the adoption order instead of a court.

Fact 3.3 Follow-up visits after adoption
After the adoption order, a social worker visits the family every 6 months for 2 years, which means 4 visits in total. The follow-up visit fee is Rs 2,000 per visit, so Rs 8,000 for all 4 visits.

Fact 3.3 Waiting list and seniority
After the home study, the parent waits in a queue. The order of the queue is called seniority and is based on the date of registration and document submission. For a healthy young child the average wait is about 3 to 3.5 years. Parents who are open to older children, siblings or children with special needs usually wait less, because most children available for adoption are older or have special needs.

Fact 3.3 Child declared legally free for adoption
The Child Welfare Committee (CWC) declares a child legally free for adoption. For an abandoned child this happens after 2 or 4 months, depending on the child's age. For a surrendered child this happens after a 60-day period in which the birth parent may change their decision.

Fact 3.2 Who is eligible to adopt
A married couple must have been in a stable marriage for at least 2 years, and both partners must agree to the adoption. A single woman can adopt a child of any gender. A single man cannot adopt a girl child. There must be an age difference of at least 25 years between the child and each parent. A couple who already has 3 or more children cannot adopt, except a child with special needs, a hard-to-place child, a relative's child or a step-child. Parents must be physically, mentally and financially able to care for a child, must not have a life-threatening illness, and must not have been convicted of a crime.

Fact 3.2 Age limits for adoption
For a couple the law uses the composite age, which is the husband's age plus the wife's age. For a single parent it uses that person's own age. For a child up to 2 years: composite age up to 85, single parent up to 40. Above 2 and up to 4 years: composite up to 90, single up to 45. Above 4 and up to 8 years: composite up to 100, single up to 50. Above 8 and up to 18 years: composite up to 110, single up to 55. Example: a husband aged 44 and a wife aged 42 have a composite age of 86, so they cannot adopt a child up to 2 years old, but they can adopt a child above 2 years.

Schedule VI: Required documents
The documents needed are: a recent family photograph and passport-size photographs; identity proof (PAN card, Aadhaar card, passport or voter ID); birth or age proof for each parent; residence proof (Aadhaar card, voter ID, passport, latest electricity bill or telephone bill); income proof (salary slip, income certificate or last year's Income Tax Return); a medical certificate from a registered medical practitioner stating that the parent has no chronic, contagious or fatal disease and is fit to adopt; a marriage certificate for married couples; a divorce decree or death certificate of the spouse for divorced or widowed applicants; reference letters from relatives or acquaintances; and the consent of older children if the family already has a child aged 5 years or above. A rent agreement is not in the usual list of accepted residence proofs; upload an accepted document, or the social worker will review it.

Schedule XV: Official fees
The Home Study Report fee is Rs 6,000. The adoption fee is Rs 50,000 for one child, plus Rs 10,000 for each additional sibling. The follow-up fee is Rs 2,000 per visit for 4 visits, Rs 8,000 in total. The total for one child is about Rs 64,000. Fees are non-refundable (CARA direction, January 2024). Any request for extra money to speed up an adoption is a warning sign of fraud.

Regulation 54: Relative adoption
In a relative adoption, the District Child Protection Unit checks the case, CARA gives a pre-approval, and then the District Magistrate issues the order. The usual age limits do not apply. A child aged 5 or above must give consent.

Regulation 55: Step-parent adoption
A step-parent adoption goes directly to the District Magistrate.

Fact 3.7 Immediate placement
A child who could not be placed through the normal queue enters a 7-day list from which Indian parents can reserve directly.

Section 80 of the Juvenile Justice Act: Illegal adoption
Adoption outside the legal process is a crime, punishable with up to 3 years in prison. In India, legal adoption happens only through CARA, the CARINGS website and registered agencies. Buying or selling a child is a crime. Fake "baby for adoption" posts on social media are scams; report them to Childline 1098 or the police.

Section 74 of the Juvenile Justice Act: Child identity
A child's identity, such as name, photo, school or address, must not be disclosed. That is why no child's photo or name is shown publicly and only a case code is used.

Fact 3.1 Organisations in adoption
CARA, the Central Adoption Resource Authority, is the national body under the Ministry of Women and Child Development; it makes the adoption rules and runs the CARINGS website. SARA is the State Adoption Resource Agency. An SAA is a Specialised Adoption Agency, an approved agency that looks after children and handles adoptions; about 795 SAAs were approved as of July 2026. The DCPU is the District Child Protection Unit. The CWC, the Child Welfare Committee, declares a child legally free for adoption. The CMO, the Chief Medical Officer, certifies whether a child is normal or has special needs. The District Magistrate issues the final adoption order.
